Mechanically, the robot utilizes the standard “Boe-Bot” chassis with two driven wheels and a rear ball caster for stability. The major addition to the design is the U-shaped 3D-printed block guard that maximizes the robot's range in collecting and storing the cubes and also the servo mechanism, spinning the pipe cleaner attached to the metal rod. The pipe cleaner bristles spin to actively sweep cubes inward and prevent them from escaping. The brush and guard features are both essential for retaining the cubes during aggressive lateral sweeps.

We discussed and explored alternative manufacturing methods for building the top and bottom guards. In the end, we settled with the idea of using 3D printed parts as they are fully customizable to ensure our design obeys the rules and it is also durable and light. An initial concern was the pricing of the 3D printed components, but we mitigated these concerns by redesigning our components to be lighter and using low infill % to decrease the mass of the printed components. These design changes are visible in the differences between figure 4: “Final U-Shaped Block Guard” CAD with sliced mass estimate used in BOM and figure 5: “Prototype Block Guard” CAD with sliced mass estimate used in BOM. We used around 7% infill for each of our components because it would be rigid enough to support blocks and light enough to cost less. An initial CAD of the guard and sweeper were used to complete milestone 1 to demonstrate our idea, but the CAD and physical components were not finalized until Milestone 4 when we needed to demonstrate their ability to collect blocks.
